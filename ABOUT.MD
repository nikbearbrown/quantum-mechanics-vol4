# About Quantum Mechanics: Information, Measurement, and Open Systems

GitHub repository: [quantum-mechanics-vol4](https://github.com/nikbearbrown/quantum-mechanics-vol4)

Author: Nik Bear Brown

## Book Summary

`quantum-mechanics-vol4` is organized as a chapter-based book project. The chapters below outline the book's main instructional arc and source files.

## Chapter Summary

1. **Chapter 1 — Mixed States and the Density Matrix** (`chapters/01-mixed-states-and-the-density-matrix.md`) - In many experiments, a system is not prepared in a single, definite quantum state.
2. **Chapter 2 — Composite Systems and Entanglement** (`chapters/02-composite-systems-and-entanglement.md`) - It is the tensor product — a four-dimensional space in which most vectors do not correspond to any product of single-particle states.
3. **Chapter 3 — Bell's Theorem and the CHSH Inequality** (`chapters/03-bells-theorem-and-chsh.md`) - Under this view, quantum particles would carry hidden instructions encoding their responses to every possible future measurement, established at the moment of creation.
4. **Chapter 4 — Quantum Gates and Circuits** (`chapters/04-quantum-gates-and-circuits.md`) - A quantum gate cannot discard information.
5. **Chapter 5 — Quantum Teleportation and Dense Coding** (`chapters/05-quantum-teleportation-and-dense-coding.md`) - *How the same entanglement that forbids copying makes movement possible.* We established in Chapter 4 that quantum states cannot be copied.
6. **Chapter 6 — Open Systems: Decoherence and the Lindblad Equation** (`chapters/06-open-systems-and-lindblad.md`) - The symbol $T_2$ appears in nearly every paper on superconducting qubits.
7. **Chapter 7 — Measurement, Interpretations, and the Quantum–Classical Boundary** (`chapters/07-measurement-and-interpretations.md`) - In 1935, Erwin Schrödinger described a thought experiment involving a cat sealed in a steel chamber with a radioactive atom, a Geiger counter, a relay, a hammer, and a flask of prussic acid.
8. **Chapter 8 — Quantum Hardware: From Formalism to Physical Qubits** (`chapters/08-quantum-hardware.md`) - In 1994, Peter Shor published a polynomial-time algorithm for factoring large integers on a quantum computer.
9. **Chapter 9 — Error and the Threshold Theorem** (`chapters/09-error-and-the-threshold-theorem.md`) - In 1994, Peter Shor published an algorithm that, run on a large enough quantum computer, would factor a 2,000-bit RSA key in hours.
10. **Chapter 10 — Capstone: Quantum Mechanics in Research** (`chapters/10-capstone-quantum-mechanics-in-research.md`) - That last skill — reading what the paper does not claim — is at least as important as reading what it does.
